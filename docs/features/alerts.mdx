---
title: alerts
description: Problems on a device's own entities, each a binary sensor that is on while the problem lasts.
---

`alerts` says when something is wrong with a device, such as a washer running for too long or a plug gone offline. Each alert watches **one** entity of the same device and becomes a binary sensor: **Problem** while its condition holds, **OK** otherwise.

pururu only **detects** the problem. Telling someone (a phone notification, a reminder, a light) is up to what reads the alert: see [Getting notified](#getting-notified).

```yaml
pururu:
  devices:
    laundry_washer:
      name: Máquina de lavar
      appliance:
        power: sensor.washer_plug_power
        running: {threshold: 4, on_delay: {minutes: 1}, off_delay: {minutes: 2}}
      alerts:
        long_cycle: {name: Ciclo longo, when: appliance_running, is: "on", for: {hours: 3}, priority: medium}
        overload: {name: Sobrecarga, when: appliance_power, above: 2500, for: {minutes: 1}, priority: high}
        plug_offline: {name: Tomada offline, when: appliance_power, is: unavailable, for: {minutes: 10}}
```

This creates `binary_sensor.pururu_laundry_washer_alert_long_cycle`, shown as **Máquina de lavar Ciclo longo**, and one alert for each other key.

## Settings

`alerts` is a map of **alert key → alert**, with at least one alert. The key is a slug, and it ends the entity ID after the namespace `alert`: `long_cycle` → `binary_sensor.pururu_laundry_washer_alert_long_cycle`.

<Property name="name" type="string" required>
  The name shown after the device's name. It can't be empty, and it's the same in every language.
</Property>

---

<Property name="when" type="entity key" required>
  The entity of **this device** the alert watches, written as its entity ID ends after the device key: `appliance_running`, `appliance_power`, `phase_current`, `switch_pump`. It must be an entity of another feature of the device, not an alert. See [Entity IDs](/concepts/entity-ids#the-pattern).
</Property>

---

<Property name="is" type="state" optional>
  The state that is a problem: `"on"`, `"off"`, a phase such as `spinning`, `unavailable`, or a number.

  - `on` and `off` work with or without quotes. Without quotes, YAML also reads `yes`/`true` as `on` and `no`/`false` as `off`.
  - A number without quotes is compared as a number: `is: 1` matches a state of `1`, `1.0` or `1.00`. In quotes (`is: "1"`) it's compared as text.
</Property>

---

<Property name="above, below" type="number" optional>
  A number is a problem when it's strictly above `above` and strictly below `below`. Give one or both; `above` must be lower than `below`.
</Property>

---

<Property name="for" type="time period" optional>
  How long the condition must hold before the alert turns on, such as `{minutes: 10}`. Without it, the alert turns on at once. A [time period](/features/appliance#settings).
</Property>

---

<Property name="priority" type="low | medium | high" optional>
  How serious the problem is, for whatever delivers the alert. Defaults to `low`.
</Property>

An alert needs **either** `is` **or** `above`/`below`, not both.

## When an alert is on

- **The condition holds**, for `is` when the watched state equals it, for `above`/`below` when the watched state is a number in the range. The alert turns on after `for`.
- **The condition stops holding:** the alert turns off at once. If it stops before `for` passes, the count starts over the next time it holds.
- **The watched entity has no reading** (`unknown`, `unavailable`, or not a number for `above`/`below`): the alert **keeps its state**, and a count in progress starts over when readings return. A washer's alert doesn't clear because its plug dropped off Wi-Fi for a minute.
- **An alert about the reading itself**, `is: unavailable` or `is: unknown`, holds while the entity has no reading: `unavailable`, `unknown`, or gone. The two mean the same, because a plug reconnecting shows `unknown` for a moment between `unavailable` and its first value, and that mustn't restart the count. So it turns on for a plug gone offline.
- **After a restart or reload,** the alert comes back as it was, and follows the watched entity again once Home Assistant has started: entities pass through `unavailable` while it starts, and that raises no alert. A condition that holds starts its `for` over.

## Entity

| Entity | What it is |
|---|---|
| `binary_sensor.pururu_<key>_alert_<alert key>` | On while the problem lasts. Device class `problem`. |

Its attributes are **`priority`** and **`watches`**, the entity it watches. It's in the device, so it's in the device's area.

## Needs

An alert needs the entity it watches. If that entity isn't created (its ID is already taken, or the device's settings don't create it, as `appliance_runtime_month` without `statistics`), the alert isn't created either, and the log says why. If you rename the watched entity in the UI, the alert follows it.

## Getting notified

pururu doesn't send notifications yet. Anything that reads a binary sensor can:

**An automation**, triggered by the alert turning on.

**Home Assistant's [`alert`](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/alert/)**, pointed at one alert:

```yaml
alert:
  washer_long_cycle:
    name: Máquina de lavar Ciclo longo
    entity_id: binary_sensor.pururu_laundry_washer_alert_long_cycle
    repeat: 30
    notifiers: [mobile_app_phone]
```

**[Alert2](https://github.com/redstone99/hass-alert2)**, with one `generator` that turns every pururu alert into an Alert2 alert, including the ones you add later:

```yaml
alert2:
  alerts:
    - generator_name: pururu
      generator: "{{ states.binary_sensor | entity_regex('binary_sensor\\.pururu_(.+_alert_.+)$') | list }}"
      domain: pururu
      name: "{{ genGroups[0] }}"
      condition: "{{ is_state(genEntityId, 'on') }}"
      friendly_name: "{{ state_attr(genEntityId, 'friendly_name') }}"
      priority: "{{ state_attr(genEntityId, 'priority') }}"
```

Each Alert2 alert is named after the rest of the pururu alert's ID, without splitting it into device and alert: `binary_sensor.pururu_laundry_washer_alert_long_cycle` becomes `alert2.pururu_laundry_washer_alert_long_cycle`, whatever the device and alert keys contain. See Alert2's documentation for its notifier, reminder and acknowledgement settings.
