---
title: alerts
description: Problems on a device's own entities, each a binary sensor that is on while the problem lasts.
---

`alerts` says when something is wrong with a device, such as a washer running for too long or a plug gone offline. Each alert watches **one** entity of the same device and becomes a binary sensor: **Problem** while its condition holds, **OK** otherwise.

An alert is for what you find **critical**, and it lasts until it's resolved. For simple news told once, such as the washer finishing, use a [notification](/concepts/notifications).

For the common cases, a feature may offer **ready-made alerts** you enable with one key: see the [appliance's](/features/appliance#ready-made-alerts).

pururu **detects** the problem and, with `notify`, says what to tell. [Alert2](https://github.com/redstone99/hass-alert2) does the telling (phones, reminders, acknowledging): see [Getting notified](#getting-notified).

```yaml
pururu:
  devices:
    laundry_washer:
      name: Máquina de lavar
      appliance:
        power: sensor.washer_plug_power
        running: {threshold: 4, on_delay: {minutes: 1}, off_delay: {minutes: 2}}
      alerts:
        long_cycle:
          name: Ciclo longo
          when: appliance_running
          is: "on"
          for: {hours: 3}
          priority: medium
          notify:
            message: A máquina está rodando há mais de 3h!
            done_message: A máquina terminou.
        overload: {name: Sobrecarga, when: appliance_power, above: 2500, for: {minutes: 1}, priority: high}
        plug_offline: {name: Tomada offline, when: appliance_power, is: unavailable, for: {minutes: 10}}
```

This creates `binary_sensor.pururu_laundry_washer_alert_long_cycle`, shown as **Máquina de lavar Ciclo longo**, and one alert for each other key.

## Settings

`alerts` is a map of **alert key → alert**, with at least one alert. The key is a slug, and it ends the entity ID after the namespace `alert`: `long_cycle` → `binary_sensor.pururu_laundry_washer_alert_long_cycle`.

<Property name="name" type="string" required>
  The name shown after the device's name. It can't be empty, and it's the same in every language.
</Property>

---

<Property name="when" type="entity key" required>
  The entity of **this device** the alert watches, written as its entity ID ends after the device key: `appliance_running`, `appliance_power`, `phase_current`, `switch_pump`. It must be an entity of another feature of the device, not an alert. See [Entity IDs](/concepts/entity-ids#the-pattern).
</Property>

---

<Property name="is" type="state" optional>
  The state that is a problem: `"on"`, `"off"`, a phase such as `spinning`, `unavailable`, or a number.

  - `on` and `off` work with or without quotes. Without quotes, YAML also reads `yes`/`true` as `on` and `no`/`false` as `off`.
  - A number without quotes is compared as a number: `is: 1` matches a state of `1`, `1.0` or `1.00`. In quotes (`is: "1"`) it's compared as text.
</Property>

---

<Property name="above, below" type="number" optional>
  A number is a problem when it's strictly above `above` and strictly below `below`. Give one or both; `above` must be lower than `below`.
</Property>

---

<Property name="for" type="time period" optional>
  How long the condition must hold before the alert turns on, such as `{minutes: 10}`. Without it, the alert turns on at once. A [time period](/features/appliance#settings).
</Property>

---

<Property name="priority" type="low | medium | high" optional>
  How serious the problem is, for whatever delivers the alert. Defaults to `low`.
</Property>

---

<Property name="notify" type="map" optional>
  What to tell, for Alert2 to deliver: `message` when the alert fires and `done_message` when it's resolved. Both are required and can't be empty. Without `notify`, Alert2 leaves the alert alone. See [Getting notified](#getting-notified).
</Property>

---

<Property name="lights" type="true | group" optional>
  Lights that show the alert while it's on: `true` for the group `default`, or a group's name, from `config: alerts: lights: groups`. See [Alert lights](/concepts/alert-lights).
</Property>

An alert needs **either** `is` **or** `above`/`below`, not both.

## When an alert is on

- **The condition holds**, for `is` when the watched state equals it, for `above`/`below` when the watched state is a number in the range. The alert turns on after `for`.
- **The condition stops holding:** the alert turns off at once. If it stops before `for` passes, the count starts over the next time it holds.
- **The watched entity has no reading** (`unknown`, `unavailable`, or not a number for `above`/`below`): the alert **keeps its state**, and a count in progress starts over when readings return. A washer's alert doesn't clear because its plug dropped off Wi-Fi for a minute.
- **An alert about the reading itself**, `is: unavailable` or `is: unknown`, holds while the entity has no reading: `unavailable`, `unknown`, or gone. The two mean the same, because a plug reconnecting shows `unknown` for a moment between `unavailable` and its first value, and that mustn't restart the count. So it turns on for a plug gone offline.
- **After a restart or reload,** the alert comes back as it was, and follows the watched entity again once Home Assistant has started: entities pass through `unavailable` while it starts, and that raises no alert. A condition that holds starts its `for` over.

## Entity

| Entity | What it is |
|---|---|
| `binary_sensor.pururu_<key>_alert_<alert key>` | On while the problem lasts. Device class `problem`. |

Its attributes are **`priority`** and **`watches`**, the entity it watches; with `notify`, **`message`** and **`done_message`**; with `lights`, **`lights`**, the name of the group it borrows. It's in the device, so it's in the device's area.

## Needs

An alert needs the entity it watches. If that entity isn't created (its ID is already taken, or the device's settings don't create it, as `appliance_runtime_month` without `statistics`), the alert isn't created either, and the log says why. If you rename the watched entity in the UI, the alert follows it.

## Getting notified

pururu sends nothing itself. [Alert2](https://github.com/redstone99/hass-alert2) delivers the alerts that have `notify`. pururu writes an Alert2 alert for each of them to `pururu/alert2/alerts.yaml`, next to `configuration.yaml`, and Alert2 reads that folder through **one line** you add once, as the `alerts` of your `alert2:` block:

```yaml title="configuration.yaml"
alert2:
  defaults:
    notifier: mobile_app_phone         # who is told
    reminder_frequency_mins: [8, 20]   # while the problem lasts
  alerts: !include_dir_merge_list pururu/alert2
```

Then restart Home Assistant, or reload Alert2 in **Developer tools → YAML → Alert2**. From then on pururu reloads Alert2 itself whenever its alerts change: adding or removing `notify`, a new alert, a changed message.

For the `long_cycle` alert above, pururu writes:

```yaml
- domain: pururu
  name: laundry_washer_alert_long_cycle
  friendly_name: Máquina de lavar Ciclo longo
  condition_on: "{{ is_state('binary_sensor.pururu_laundry_washer_alert_long_cycle', 'on') }}"
  condition_off: "{{ is_state('binary_sensor.pururu_laundry_washer_alert_long_cycle', 'off') }}"
  priority: medium
  message: A máquina está rodando há mais de 3h!
  done_message: A máquina terminou.
```

- **What each alert says** comes from its `notify`. **How** it's told is the same for every alert, in Alert2's `defaults`: who is told, reminders, acknowledging. See Alert2's documentation.
- Each Alert2 alert is named after the rest of the pururu alert's ID: `binary_sensor.pururu_laundry_washer_alert_long_cycle` becomes `alert2.pururu_laundry_washer_alert_long_cycle`, even after you rename the pururu alert.
- Only the alerts with `notify` are written, and only those that are created.
- A message or name with `{` is shown as you wrote it, not as a template.
- `condition_on` and `condition_off`, rather than one `condition`, keep the Alert2 alert as it is while the pururu alert is briefly `unavailable`, as during a pururu reload.
- Until the line is there, **Settings → Repairs** shows **Alert2 doesn't run pururu's alerts**, with the line to add. See [Troubleshooting](/reference/troubleshooting#alert2-doesnt-run-pururus-alerts).
- If an alert has `notify` and Alert2 isn't set up once Home Assistant has started, the log says so: `binary_sensor.pururu_laundry_washer_alert_long_cycle has notify, but Alert2 isn't set up to deliver it`. The alert still works as a binary sensor.

<Warning>
  `alerts:` is the include alone: a YAML list can't be both written out and included. Create **your own** Alert2 alerts in Alert2's UI instead. Don't put files in `pururu/alert2` and don't edit `alerts.yaml`: the include loads every file there, and pururu rewrites it whenever the configuration changes.
</Warning>

### Coming from the generator

pururu 0.1.11 and earlier asked for a `generator` in Alert2's `alerts`. Replace it with the line above. The alerts keep their Alert2 entities, history and acknowledgements: they have the same names. Kept next to the include, the generator declares every alert twice, and Alert2 reports `Duplicate declaration of alert for domain=pururu`.

**Without `notify`**, the alert is yours to use: an automation triggered by it turning on, or Home Assistant's [`alert`](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/alert/):

```yaml
alert:
  washer_long_cycle:
    name: Máquina de lavar Ciclo longo
    entity_id: binary_sensor.pururu_laundry_washer_alert_long_cycle
    repeat: 30
    notifiers: [mobile_app_phone]
```
