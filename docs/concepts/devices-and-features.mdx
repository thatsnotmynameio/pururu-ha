title: Devices and features
description: A pururu device is a key, a name and one or more features, and the features create its entities.

## A device

Every entry under `devices:` is one device in Home Assistant:

```yaml
pururu:
  areas:
    despensa: {name: Despensa}
  devices:
    clothes_washer:          # the key
      name: Tanquinho # required
      area: despensa       # optional: a key of areas:
      appliance:             # one or more features
        power: homeassistant.sensor.washer_plug_power
        running_program:
          above: 4
          on_delay: {minutes: 1}
          off_delay: {minutes: 2}
          phases:
            warming: {name: Aquecendo, above: 1000}
```

- **The key** (`clothes_washer`) identifies the device. It must be a slug: lowercase letters, digits and `_`. It becomes part of every entity ID, `sensor.pururu_clothes_washer_…`, and it's how pururu recognises the device across reloads. If you rename the key, pururu treats it as a new device: the old one and its entities are deleted, and the new one starts with empty history and counters.
- **`name`** is the name Home Assistant shows. It can't be empty. Change it whenever you like. Renaming the device in the UI works too, and pururu keeps your name.
- **`area`** puts the device in one of the areas declared under `areas:`. It's optional, and it's covered in [Floors and areas](/concepts/floors-and-areas#a-device-in-an-area).
- **`reactions`** makes the device react to events: each one becomes a Home Assistant automation. It's optional, it isn't a feature, and it's covered in [Reactions](/concepts/reactions).
- **`programs`** gives the device [executable programs](/concepts/programs#executable-programs), under `programs: executable:`: each one becomes a Home Assistant script. It's optional, and it isn't a feature. A feature's block takes its own detected programs instead (`programs: detected:` in the [appliance](/features/appliance)'s).
- **`alerts`** watches for problems on the device's own entities: each one becomes a binary sensor. It's optional, it isn't a feature either, and it's covered in [Alerts](/concepts/alerts).
- **`goals`** says what the device must achieve in each period, such as hours of filtering a day: each one becomes two sensors, its target and what was done. It's optional, it isn't a feature either, and it's covered in [Goals](/concepts/goals).
- **Features** are the other keys. A device needs at least one; `reactions`, `programs`, `alerts` and `goals` don't count towards it.

## A feature

A feature is a ready-made piece of behaviour. It reads real entities, such as a plug's power sensor, and applies a few settings, such as a band of its power, or, for [`switches`](/features/switches) and [`lights`](/features/lights), passes commands on to them. From those it creates the device's entities, and pururu keeps them up to date.

| Feature | What it does |
|---|---|
| [`appliance`](/features/appliance) | An appliance on a power-measuring plug: when it runs, its cycles, their duration and energy, runtime and cycle counts per period, and, with phases, what it's doing during a cycle |
| [`door`](/features/door) | A door from its contact sensor: each opening, their count and time open per period, and, with events, who opened it, how and which way |
| [`window`](/features/window) | A window from its contact sensor, as a door |
| [`lights`](/features/lights) | Real lights, or relays that drive a lamp, inside the device, each named in the YAML, that show their light's state, offer what it offers and pass commands on |
| [`switches`](/features/switches) | Real switches inside the device, each named in the YAML, that show their switch's state and turn it on and off |
| [`buttons`](/features/buttons) | A value of a real sensor, such as a remote's key, as a button of the device: a press records its time and can start one of the device's programs |

A device can also have [`reactions`](/concepts/reactions), [`programs`](/concepts/programs#executable-programs) (its executable programs), [`alerts`](/concepts/alerts) and [`goals`](/concepts/goals). None of them are features, and a device still needs a feature besides them: `reactions` and `programs` write Home Assistant automations and scripts, each executable program's runs and each reaction's triggers a sensor of the device; `alerts` watches other features' entities (and the executable programs' and reactions' statistics) for problems, each one a binary sensor of the device too; `goals` measures a total of the device, of another device or of Home Assistant's against a target per period, each goal two sensors of the device.

Each feature appears at most once per device, and everything under its key is that feature's configuration. An unknown key inside a feature, such as a typo, is a configuration error, so a mistake never passes silently.

## Every feature stands alone

A feature reads only the real entities and settings in its own block; it never reads another feature's. What used to be split into separate `phases` and `modes` features, each following another feature's cycle, is now the appliance's own `running_program`: its `phases` are bands of the same power the appliance already reads. A detected program of its own, `programs: detected:`, sits in the same block and reads the same power. See [Programs](/concepts/programs#detected-programs).

## Naming an entity in the YAML

An alert, a program's step or a reaction names an entity by its **path**: the keys you wrote, from the block in the device down to the entity, each separated by a dot. In the device above, the washer running is `appliance.running_program`, the `warming` phase `appliance.running_program.phases.warming`, and the power `appliance.power`. Another device's entity starts with `device.` and its key (`device.clothes_washer.appliance.running_program`), and an entity of Home Assistant's with `homeassistant.` (`homeassistant.sensor.washer_plug_power`, as `power` writes it). See [References](/concepts/entity-ids#references).

## Two devices, one plug?

Each device is independent, so two devices can read the same plug. For example, one device can count cycles with a low band while another uses a higher one. Their entity IDs never collide, because each one carries its device's key.
